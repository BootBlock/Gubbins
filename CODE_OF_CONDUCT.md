# Code of conduct

Gubbins is a small project with a single maintainer. This page exists so that the standard
expected of everyone taking part is written down rather than assumed, and so that anyone
dealing with unacceptable behaviour knows where to take it.

It applies to everyone equally — the maintainer included.

## The standard

Be civil and assume good faith. People here have different levels of experience, different
first languages, and different amounts of time to give. Most friction is a misunderstanding
rather than malice.

**Expected:**

- Keep discussion focused on the work — the code, the bug, the idea.
- Accept that a reasonable request may still be declined, and that a decision can be revisited
  without it being reopened indefinitely.
- Respect that maintainer time is finite and unpaid. Nobody here is on call.

**Not acceptable, in any venue covered below:**

- Harassment of any kind, including sustained unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Personal attacks, insults, or demeaning remarks — including about someone's background,
  identity, beliefs, or level of skill.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information — an address, a private email, a workplace, a real
  name tied to a pseudonymous account — without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or persistently pressing a point that has been answered.

This is not an exhaustive list, and "it wasn't technically on the list" is not a defence.

## Where it applies

Every space this project runs: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code and
comments, and the wiki. It also applies when someone is representing the project elsewhere —
for example, posting as a maintainer or using an official project account.

It does **not** extend to unrelated conduct in other communities. What happens elsewhere is
that community's business, not this one's.

## Reporting

**For anything serious — harassment, threats, or targeting of an individual — report it to
GitHub directly:** <https://github.com/contact/report-abuse>.

That route is deliberately first, and it is not a brush-off. GitHub's Trust & Safety team is
staffed, handles reports privately, and can act on accounts across the whole platform. This
project has one maintainer, no shared inbox, and no private email address that can receive
reports — the `@users.noreply.github.com` identity used for commits cannot receive mail. It
would be dishonest to publish a contact channel here that might sit unread for a week.

For something minor and non-sensitive — a comment that has crossed a line, a thread turning
unpleasant — opening an issue or replying in the thread and tagging **@BootBlock** is fine.

Do not use the security reporting route in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for conduct matters; it
is for vulnerabilities.

## What happens next

Reports are taken seriously and handled as privately as the situation allows. Given the size of
the project, a response may not be immediate — that is a statement of capacity, not of how much
it matters. If a report needs faster action than one person can give, GitHub's abuse route
above is the better path.

The response will be proportionate to what happened. Depending on severity, that may mean any
of: a private word, editing or removing the offending content, locking a thread, or blocking
the account from the repository. Serious or repeated behaviour goes straight to a block and a
report to GitHub. Where someone has clearly acted in good faith and simply got it wrong, the
expected outcome is a correction and moving on — not a punishment.

Anyone who reports something in good faith will not be penalised for it, whatever the outcome.
Deliberately false or retaliatory reports are themselves a breach of this policy.

## A note on scale

Realistically, this project is small enough that none of the above may ever be needed. It is
written down anyway, because the moment it *is* needed is the worst possible moment to be
working out what the rules are.
