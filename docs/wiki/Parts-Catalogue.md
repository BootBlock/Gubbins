# Parts catalogue

The **parts catalogue** is a branded, printable catalogue of your inventory — a clean document
listing your items with their details, ready to print or share as a PDF.

**Where to find it:** the **Reports** screen.

## What it produces

The catalogue lays your items out as a formatted document — names, key details and images — in a
tidy, readable style suited to **print**. Use it to:

- Hand someone a browsable list of what you have.
- Keep an offline paper reference of your stock.
- Produce a simple catalogue of parts or a collection.

Like the [[insurance schedule|Insurance-and-Estate-Schedule]], it's **print-styled**: printing it
(→ Save as PDF) drops the app chrome and switches to a clean layout, using your browser's own
print — no extra software.

> **ℹ️ Note**
> The catalogue is generated locally and only leaves your device when *you* print or save it. See
> [[Privacy & security|Privacy-and-Security]].

## Choosing what goes in

**Show** at the top of the screen decides which items the catalogue covers:

- **All items** — your whole active inventory.
- **By location** — a [[location|Locations-and-Stock]] *and everything nested inside it*, so a
  catalogue for a room also covers its shelves and bins.
- **By project** — every item on a [[project's|Projects-and-BOM]] bill of materials.
- **Selected items** — the items you ticked in the inventory before coming here.

A narrower selection is usually the more useful document — a single collection, one room, or one
supplier's parts — and it is also what to reach for if the whole inventory turns out to be too big
to print (below).

## How lines are valued

With the money columns switched on, each line shows a **unit cost** and a **value** (unit cost ×
what's on hand), and every section carries a subtotal beneath a grand total at the end.

A line is priced by the same valuation as the rest of Gubbins: an item's manual
**[[current value|Current-Value-and-Revaluation]]**, else its unit cost, else its preferred
supplier's price, else its purchase price less [[depreciation|Warranty-and-Depreciation]] — or, for
a [[consumable gauge|Low-Stock-and-Gauges]], what it holds × its *cost per unit of measure*. So a
revalued collectible is catalogued at today's worth, and the figure agrees with the one on the
[[insurance schedule|Insurance-and-Estate-Schedule]].

An item **nothing** prices reads `—` in both columns rather than a misleading `0.00`, and adds
nothing to the totals.

## Printing

Beside the **Print / Save as PDF** button, Gubbins shows how big the job is: how many items the
catalogue covers, and roughly how many printed pages that comes to. It is an estimate — your paper
size, your printer's margins and how much of each column's text wraps all move it — but it tells a
two-page parts list from a two-hundred-page one before you commit to anything.

Ask for a long print and Gubbins checks with you first, so a whole-inventory catalogue is never a
surprise waiting at the printer.

> **💡 Tip**
> The **Photo** and **QR** columns make every row several times taller. Turning them off is the
> quickest way to shorten a long catalogue — and the only way to keep a large one printable at all.

### Very large catalogues

Printing has a size limit, because a very large catalogue runs to thousands of pages. Above roughly
**20,000 items** — or **2,000** with the **Photo** or **QR** column on, since each of those has to
be built for every single row — the catalogue isn't produced and the Print button is switched off.
Narrow the selection to a location or a project, or turn those columns off, and it comes straight
back.

> **ℹ️ Note**
> The [[insurance schedule|Insurance-and-Estate-Schedule]] draws the same line, for the same
> reason. If you need a document of everything at that size, export your data as a file instead
> (see [[Export & import|Export-and-Import]]) — a file can be searched, re-sorted and printed a
> part at a time.
